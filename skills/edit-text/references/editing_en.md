# Edit natural prose

Read when editing or when your own draft sounds formulaic. These are decision aids, not a banned-word dictionary or an authorship detector.

## Preserve the substance first

Separate the facts, the author's stance and the purpose of the text from its presentation.
Check quantities against their units and referents. Preserve addresses, links,
attribution, conditions, deadlines and the author's degree of certainty.

Check who acted, the sequence of events and which causal claims the source supports.
When shortening, omit secondary details. Retain material limits on the claims
you keep, the offer itself and any action the reader needs to take.

## Check the opening and its promise

Read a standalone piece's title or first sentence without neighboring posts: are the subject and reason to continue clear? Replace a generic introduction with a supported detail, consequence, dilemma or recognizable scene. You may delay the explanation or ending, but not hide the subject or material conditions.

Compare the opening with the rest: does it provide the promised explanation, example, criterion or emotional point? Preserve the original degree of certainty and scope of the claim. When the material is thin, narrow the promise or clarify an essential gap instead of inventing experience, numbers or achievements.

Check whether a closing question develops the content and is needed at all. Remove reaction bait and manufactured arguments; retain a specific question that helps explore the subject. For multiple results, compare openings, rhythm and endings, varying them as the content requires. A good opening need not be replaced with a new formula.

## Common problems

| Problem | Decision |
|---|---|
| Empty introduction about the modern world | Start with what happened or what the author observed |
| Importance without substance | Name a consequence supported by the materials, or remove the claim |
| "Not just…" without a real objection | State the point directly; retain a meaningful comparison |
| Unnamed experts or studies | Identify an accessible source, or preserve it as someone's unverified claim |
| Several paragraphs repeating a point | Keep what is needed rather than disguise repetition with synonyms |
| Bureaucratic wording | Say who does what, when the actor is known |
| Press-release recap and specification dump | Explain what changes for the reader; keep details that help explain it |
| Identical hooks, triplets and endings | Rework for the content rather than substitute another template |
| A moral that repeats the whole piece | End on the last useful detail |
| "Honestly," "well," "basically" in every paragraph | Remove forced casualness unless it is the author's voice |
| Assistant chatter and interface markers | Remove them from the draft while preserving real links and attribution |

Follow the [punctuation guidance for authored prose](../SKILL_en.md#procedure).
Keep purposeful repetition, questions, terms and lists when they help the reader.
Preserve the author's voice in passages that already work.

## When to stop

Read the draft without editorial notes. Is it clear who is speaking, why the reader needs it and what supports its claims? If so, and no concrete problem remains, further polishing is optional. When asked to fix one aspect, stay within that scope. Compare retained meaning, omissions and certainty with the materials. Return the requested draft without an editing report unless asked for one.
