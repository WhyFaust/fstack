# Adapt to the task

## Form and structure

Use the request and preceding messages to determine where and how the reader
will encounter the text. If the deliverable is unclear and the choice changes
the work, ask one question with options.

- For speech, check that the words work aloud. Confirm an exact duration with a timed reading. Keep stage directions separate from spoken words.
- In a title or description, name the subject and give a reason to open the material. Check that it delivers what the opening promises.
- In a longer piece, provide the explanations the reader needs. Separate steps, alternatives and distinct points where doing so helps the reader.
- In a short field, retain the main point and essential qualifications. If they cannot fit, narrow the claim within scope or agree on a different format.

When adapting, preserve facts, stance, commitments and the action the reader
needs to take. Choose depth, order and formatting for the new purpose. Supply the
required parts of a standalone publication. If the request specifies one field,
return that field alone. Include offers and response mechanisms when supported
by the brief and the author's materials. For a poll, make the response method
clear and the options distinct. Allow a free response if the options are incomplete.

## Technical explanations and instructions

Introduce unfamiliar objects by their role. The reader should understand the
action and its cause from the material available to them. Verify each added
explanation as a factual claim. Check quantitative relationships or disputed
wording against a boundary case. Retain the code, depth and terminology the
intended audience needs.

- Identify the actor and affected object when known. Name each concept consistently and resolve ambiguous references.
- Preserve sequence, conditions, consequences and certainty. Check the basis of causal claims. Limit conclusions to the scope of the evidence.
- Place prerequisites and established risks before the affected action. Number steps when their sequence matters.
- Check figures, units, negations, constraints and guarantees against the source. Preserve significant wording, code, commands, paths, identifiers, error messages and exact quotations verbatim.

## Conversational replies

Identify the recipient and parent comment from the thread relationships and
check later clarifications. Ask for missing text if it affects the reply.
Address the specific point in a way that fits the relationship and the author's
stance. Choose the length, tone and any follow-up question according to what
the reply contributes to the conversation.

When disagreeing, address the claim and give the reason. Consider the sensitivity
of the situation. Ground the author's experience, views, authority, deadlines and
completed actions in confirmed material. Clarify any missing information or
authority with the author before promising a next step. Keep requests for
personal information out of public threads.

## Destination constraints

Check community and field rules that affect content, placement and formatting.
Select required categories from available options. Explain any rule that would
exclude the proposed material and offer a permitted form. Claim compliance
only with rules you could verify.

For an exact limit, use current documentation or a counter for the specific
field. Use its unit and count everything that will go in the field, including
links. If you cannot verify the exact length, disclose this outside the draft.
